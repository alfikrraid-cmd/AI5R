# Memory

Memory stores organizational experiences.

Memory answers:

"What happened?"

Memory SHALL NOT create organizational knowledge.

Examples:

- Customer complained.
- Campaign executed.
- Meeting completed.
- Proposal rejected.

---

# Evidence

Evidence aggregates memories.

Evidence answers:

"What pattern is emerging?"

Evidence SHALL contain measurable information.

Examples:

- Success Rate
- Failure Rate
- Confidence Score
- Sample Size
- Trend
- KPI History

Evidence SHALL remain objective.

Evidence SHALL NOT contain conclusions.

---

# Knowledge

Knowledge represents validated organizational understanding.

Knowledge answers:

"What has been sufficiently proven?"

Knowledge SHALL be derived from validated evidence.

Knowledge SHALL NOT be created directly from memory.

---

# Knowledge Promotion

Knowledge lifecycle:

Memory

↓

Evidence

↓

Knowledge Candidate

↓

Validation

↓

Approved Knowledge

Only Approved Knowledge may influence organizational decisions.

---

# Evidence Principle

Evidence SHALL include:

- Sample Size
- Success Count
- Failure Count
- Confidence Score
- Measurement Period
- Supporting Artifacts

---

# Knowledge Types

Canonical Knowledge

Examples:

- Accounting Standards
- Laws
- Scientific Principles

Organizational Knowledge

Examples:

- SOP
- Internal Policies
- Company Best Practices

Learned Knowledge

Examples:

- Marketing Patterns
- Operational Improvements
- Sales Strategies

---

# Decision Principle

Decision Engines SHALL consume:

- Canonical Knowledge
- Organizational Knowledge
- Learned Knowledge

Decision Engines SHALL NOT consume raw Memory.

---

# Artifact Principle

Execution produces Artifacts.

Artifacts are business deliverables.

Examples:

- Business Plan
- Campaign
- Financial Report
- SOP
- Contract
- Presentation

Artifacts SHALL become inputs for future Evidence.

---

# Constitution Amendment A-02

Principle:

No organizational knowledge SHALL be created solely from memory.

Knowledge SHALL be derived from validated evidence.
